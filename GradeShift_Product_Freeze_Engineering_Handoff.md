## Product freeze rules

1. The POC has one primary decision: **commercial disposition during one A→B transition**.
2. The primary user is the **board/operator plus quality approver**, not an autonomous controller.
3. The minimum decision vocabulary is:

   - **SAMPLE NOW**
   - **PRIME-RELEASE CANDIDATE**
   - **ABSTAIN / FOLLOW SOP**
4. “TRANSITION SILO” may be shown as the current route, not as an AI-controlled valve command.

10. No HMEL-specific performance or savings claim is presented as measured without HMEL data.

# 3. FINAL PRODUCT DEFINITION

## The exact product

PrimePath consumes time-aligned process, lab, recipe, production, and routing context for an active grade transition. It estimates the quality state and calibrated uncertainty of the material associated with the current production window; checks data health and applicability; maps reactor state to downstream material identity; and recommends the lowest-expected-loss allowed action.

Its output is not “MFI = 7.9.” Its output is:

- estimated quality and prediction interval;
- specification and dwell status;
- material window affected;
- sensor-health and OOD status;
- analogous historical transitions;
- expected cost of holding, acting, and sampling;
- recommendation;
- reason codes and evidence;
- required approver;
- fallback if the recommendation is rejected or the system abstains.

## Definition of “prime”

PrimePath distinguishes four states:

1. **Estimated reactor quality in specification:** a model state, not a commercial release.
2. **Associated material window likely in specification:** includes residence time and transport alignment.
3. **Prime-release candidate:** model evidence, uncertainty, health, support, dwell, and workflow rules satisfy the configured advisory policy.
4. **Commercially released/certified material:** a human and quality-system outcome governed by HMEL SOP/LIMS. PrimePath records it but does not create it.

## Users and decisions

| User | Product use |
|---|---|
| Board/operator | See current candidate disposition, reason, confidence, material window, and fallback |
| Quality/QC | Prioritize a sample, review interval/spec evidence, approve or reject candidate release |
| Process engineer | Review trajectory, residuals, sensor health, OOD, and comparable episodes |
| Plant manager | See avoidable hold/downgrade, false-prime protection, and realized value |
| Manufacturing head | Compare units/grade pairs using normalized, evidence-labelled KPIs |
| Planning | Use transition history and observed disposition delays; no automatic schedule optimization in the POC |

## Formal decision

For action \(a\) in the allowed action set:

\[
\operatorname{EL}(a \mid x)=
P(\text{material bad}\mid x)C_{\text{false-prime}}(a)
+P(\text{material good}\mid x)C_{\text{false-hold}}(a)
+C_{\text{sample}}(a)
+C_{\text{workflow}}(a)
\]

Choose the permitted action with minimum expected loss, subject to hard policy gates:

- valid transition and grade context;
- required features present and fresh;
- sensor health acceptable;
- no hard abnormal-state flag;
- OOD support acceptable;
- uncertainty calibration policy satisfied;
- material-identity mapping available;
- specification and dwell logic satisfied;
- required human approval retained.

If a hard gate fails, choose **ABSTAIN / FOLLOW SOP**, regardless of expected value.

For **SAMPLE NOW**, calculate expected value of information:

\[
\operatorname{VOI}=
\min_a \operatorname{EL}(a\mid x)
-\mathbb{E}_{y}\left[\min_a \operatorname{EL}(a\mid x,y)\right]
-C_{\text{sample}}
\]

Recommend a sample only when VOI exceeds the configured operational threshold and a result can arrive in time to change the decision.

## Exact white space

PrimePath is not “better APC.” It is a vendor-neutral advisory layer connecting:

**process trajectory + delayed quality truth + uncertainty + material identity + disposition workflow + asymmetric economic consequence + audited learning.**

Commercial vendors offer substantial overlapping capabilities: nonlinear/APC control, inferentials, grade-change automation, scheduling, and industrial analytics. AspenTech explicitly markets nonlinear control and polymer scheduling; Yokogawa publishes grade-change and HMEL automation work; Siemens and AVEVA offer APC; Imubit markets polymer-reactor optimization; Seeq markets transition analytics.[^https://www.aspentech.com/en/products/msc/aspen-nonlinear-controller] [^https://www.aspentech.com/en/applications/msc/polymers-scheduling] [^https://www.yokogawa.com/us/library/resources/references/improvement-of-grade-change-operation-japan-polychem-corporation-kawasaki-factory/] [^https://web-material3.yokogawa.com/1/5018/files/suc-HMEL.pdf] [^https://www.siemens.com/en-gb/company/insights/simatic-apc] [^https://www.aveva.com/en/products/apc] [^https://imubit.com/articles/polymer-reactor] [^https://www.seeq.com/resources/use-cases/chemical-reactor-product-transition-optimization/]

The defendable gap is therefore not that these systems “cannot optimize transitions.” It is that PrimePath makes the **commercial disposition decision itself** explicit, uncertainty-calibrated, cost-sensitive, human-authorized, replayable, and linked to realized outcome.

# 4. WHY THIS CAN WIN

1. **Thirty-second clarity:** “We tell the operator when transitional material is a defensible prime-release candidate, whether another sample is worth taking, and how much waiting costs.”
2. **A memorable non-obvious insight:** the expensive loss may occur after the reactor is already acceptable, because commercial routing waits for delayed and uncertain truth.
3. **It complements HMEL’s stack:** read-only historian/LIMS ingestion first; no attempt to replace DCS, APC, SIS, LIMS, or quality authority.
4. **It produces a decision:** HOLD, SAMPLE NOW, PRIME-RELEASE CANDIDATE, or ABSTAIN—not another dashboard prediction.
5. **Uncertainty changes behavior:** interval width, OOD, and sensor health directly gate actions.
6. **Safety and trust are credible:** abstention and human authorization replace the deck’s indefensible “absolute safety” guarantee.
7. **It can be demonstrated honestly:** a seeded offline replay can expose every input, prediction, interval, policy gate, recommendation, and outcome without pretending to be a plant trial.
8. **It creates a defensible learning loop:** Transition Memory links what was predicted and recommended to what the lab and final disposition later established.
9. **It differentiates without attacking incumbents:** PrimePath can sit above existing automation and use its outputs.
10. **It narrows scope:** one unit-neutral A→B replay, one decision, one holdout episode, and one economic ledger.

The jury “aha” is:

> At 10:42, the point estimate is inside specification, but PrimePath still says HOLD because the 90% interval crosses the limit. At 10:51, another sample has positive value, so it says SAMPLE NOW. At 11:08, the interval, dwell, data support, and material window all satisfy policy; it says PRIME-RELEASE CANDIDATE. The later lab result confirms the candidate. The replay shows the tonnes and margin that the current timing would have held unnecessarily—without claiming that AI certified the product.

# 5. FINAL ARCHITECTURE

## End-to-end flow

**Historian/process tags + LIMS/lab events + grade/recipe context + routing/disposition events + production/economic assumptions**

→ **Provenance and time normalization**

→ **Transition Event Builder**

→ **Material Identity / Residence-Time Mapper**

→ **Unit-Specific Quality Estimator**

→ **Calibrated Uncertainty**

→ **Sensor Health + Abnormality + OOD**

→ **Policy Gate**

→ **Expected-Loss / Value-of-Information Decision Engine**

→ **Operator Recommendation and Human Approval**

→ **Outcome Reconciliation**

→ **Transition Memory**

→ **Offline Replay / Model Assurance / Future planning evidence**

## Core services

### 1. Data contracts and provenance

Every observation carries:

- event ID, unit ID, grade-from, grade-to;
- source system and source tag;
- source timestamp, ingestion timestamp, time zone;
- raw value, engineering unit, normalized value;
- quality flag, missing/frozen/range/rate status;
- transformation version;
- assumption/source label: `MEASURED`, `PUBLICLY_VERIFIED`, `INDUSTRY_BENCHMARK`, `ASSUMPTION`, `SIMULATED`, or `ILLUSTRATIVE`.

### 2. Transition Event Builder

Creates one immutable episode with:

- transition declaration/start/end rules;
- process time series;
- commands/setpoints as observed context;
- lab sample collected-at, result-at, and result value;
- routing changes and material destinations;
- production-rate trace;
- abnormalities and interventions;
- eventual quality/disposition outcome;
- economic assumptions;
- dataset/model/policy versions.

Rows from the same transition may never be split across training and test sets.

### 3. Material Identity Mapper

Separates reactor state from material reaching a sampler or silo. For the POC, implement configurable:

- reactor residence-time kernel (CSTR or Erlang tanks-in-series);
- deterministic downstream transport delay;
- pelletizer/extruder delay;
- destination/routing intervals;
- mass integration by time window.

The mapper returns the production-time distribution associated with the material at a decision point. It is a model with uncertainty, not a perfect tracker.

### 4. Quality estimation

Use a unit-specific interface. For the POC:

- reproducible physics/lag features;
- lagged process statistics and slopes;
- time since transition and grade context;
- a transparent baseline;
- a CPU-friendly tabular estimator;
- optional residual correction.

Do not require LSTM, PINN, MoM, or EKF unless they improve locked-holdout decision metrics. The existing toy LSTM is not privileged.

### 5. Uncertainty

Use split conformal or conformalized quantile regression around a validation-frozen model. Report:

- nominal coverage;
- empirical coverage;
- interval width;
- coverage by grade direction and transition phase;
- calibration limitations under distribution shift.

Uncertainty must gate the decision; it is not a confidence decoration.

### 6. Health, abnormality, and OOD

Combine:

- deterministic missingness/freshness/frozen/range/rate checks;
- robust feature-distance or nearest-neighbour support;
- an isolation model fitted only on training data;
- unseen grade-pair/unit checks;
- disagreement/residual alarms where available.

Expose separate states: `NORMAL`, `LOW_CONFIDENCE`, `ABNORMAL`, `OOD`, `ABSTAIN`.

### 7. Disposition engine

Inputs:

- point/interval quality estimates;
- specification limits and dwell rule;
- material window;
- health/OOD gates;
- approved action policy;
- expected costs;
- analogous transition evidence.

Outputs:

- action;
- reason codes;
- disqualifying gates;
- expected-loss comparison;
- VOI;
- approver;
- expiry time;
- fallback.

### 8. Transition Memory

Stores immutable episode summaries and decision snapshots. Retrieval is directional and contextual:

- A→B is not B→A;
- unit and operating regime matter;
- only training-approved historical episodes can support a live recommendation;
- holdout episodes remain excluded from model fitting and analogy support during evaluation.

### 9. Model Assurance

Maintains:

- data manifest and hashes;
- feature, model, calibration, OOD, policy, and economic versions;
- evaluation reports;
- claim registry;
- model card;
- limitations;
- promotion checklist;
- audit log.

### 10. Optional modules

The synthetic reactor/replay engine and old optimizer may generate controlled scenarios and counterfactuals. Their outputs must be labelled `SIMULATED`. They cannot substantiate HMEL savings or process-safety claims.

# 7. TECHNICAL VALIDATION STRATEGY

## Evidence ladder

1. **Unit tests:** schemas, time alignment, no leakage, material mass conservation, decision rules, VOI, and abstention.
2. **Synthetic truth tests:** known trajectories, delays, sensor failures, OOD shifts, and decision consequences.
3. **Locked offline replay:** event-level chronological holdout never used for fitting, calibration, thresholds, or analogy retrieval.
4. **Shadow mode with HMEL data:** read-only recommendations compared with actual decisions; no operational actuation.
5. **Prospective advisory pilot:** approved operators review recommendations; quality authority remains unchanged.

The competition POC may claim only levels 1–3.

## Dataset partitions

- Partition by complete transition event, never by row.
- Prefer chronological split: earliest episodes for training, next block for model/uncertainty/OOD/policy selection, newest block locked.
- Group by directional grade pair and unit.
- Freeze seeds, manifests, hashes, transformation code, specifications, and economic assumptions before opening the locked holdout.
- Do not use holdout events as “similar historical transitions.”

## Baselines

Compare against:

1. current/SOP timing fixture;
2. last-lab carry-forward;
3. point-estimate threshold without uncertainty;
4. uncertainty-aware PrimePath;
5. optional oracle for diagnostic upper bound only.

## Metric hierarchy

### Quality estimation

- MAE and RMSE in original units;
- bias;
- time-dependent residuals;
- performance by directional grade pair and phase.

### Uncertainty

- empirical coverage at nominal 90%;
- mean/median interval width;
- under-coverage by subgroup;
- calibration curve.

### Decision and risk

- false-prime candidate rate and mass;
- false-hold rate, duration, and mass;
- sample recommendations and useful-sample fraction;
- abstention rate;
- risk-coverage curve;
- decision latency;
- expected and realized regret against oracle;
- value recovered relative to declared baseline.

### Robustness

- missing, delayed, frozen, biased, and spiking sensors;
- unseen grade direction;
- catalyst/regime shift fixture;
- changed residence time;
- late/missing lab result;
- routing-event gaps.

## Acceptance gates

- Every displayed result is reproducible from a command and data manifest.
- No training/validation/holdout event leakage.
- Nominal interval coverage is reported honestly; it is not forced by widening without a width trade-off.
- PrimePath must not create a worse false-prime risk than the declared SOP baseline on the locked replay.
- Positive value is required after false-prime, false-hold, sample, and abstention costs.
- OOD and critical sensor failures must force abstention in tests.
- The canonical demo must have no hidden manual edits or hard-coded outcome.
- If results do not support a prime-release candidate, the correct demo result is HOLD or ABSTAIN.

# 8. FINAL POC

## Scope

One reproducible, unit-neutral **Grade A→Grade B** transition using seeded synthetic data and a locked episode. All figures are labelled simulated/illustrative.

## Demo flow

1. **Provenance card:** dataset hash, episode ID, simulation label, model/calibration/policy versions.
2. **Transition start:** grade pair, specification, production rate, current route, and process context.
3. **Timeline:** process observations, sample-collected times, result-arrival times, routing intervals, and downstream material window.
4. **Current estimate:** quality point estimate plus 90% interval; no isolated vanity confidence score.
5. **Support check:** sensor health, OOD status, analogous training episodes, and any failed gate.
6. **Decision checkpoint 1:** point estimate enters spec but interval crosses the limit → **HOLD**.
7. **Decision checkpoint 2:** uncertainty remains economically important and a timely sample can change the action → **SAMPLE NOW** with VOI.
8. **Decision checkpoint 3:** interval, dwell, material mapping, health, and support satisfy policy → **PRIME-RELEASE CANDIDATE**, pending quality/operator approval.
9. **Expected-loss table:** HOLD vs SAMPLE NOW vs candidate release, including false-prime and false-hold consequences.
10. **Reveal outcome:** later simulated lab truth and recorded final disposition.
11. **Counterfactual:** compare declared SOP timing with PrimePath timing; integrate only the affected mass window.
12. **Value ledger:** tonnes, minutes, downgrade spread, sample cost, risk cost, and net value with assumptions.
13. **Memory update:** show the immutable record added after outcome reconciliation.
14. **Failure branch:** inject a frozen critical sensor or OOD regime; recommendation changes to **ABSTAIN / FOLLOW SOP**.

- correct event/time alignment;
- calibrated interval used in a decision;
- material identity represented explicitly;
- asymmetric cost and VOI;
- abstention;
- audited outcome and learning;
- reproducible counterfactual value.

## What the POC does not prove

- HMEL plant performance;
- industrial certification;
- product-quality release authority;
- control-system safety;
- ₹60–102 Cr/year savings;
- superiority across all four platforms;
- a live plant POC.

# 9. ECONOMIC PROOF

## Episode-level accounting

For each time interval \(\Delta t\):

\[
m_t=\dot m_t\Delta t
\]

\[
V_{\text{late hold}}=
\sum_{t \in \text{good but held}}m_t
(P_{\text{prime}}-P_{\text{actual route}})
\]

\[
C_{\text{false prime}}=
\sum_{t \in \text{bad but candidate}}m_t
\times C_{\text{consequence per tonne}}
\]

\[
V_{\text{net}}=
V_{\text{avoided late hold}}
-C_{\text{false prime}}
-C_{\text{additional samples}}
-C_{\text{workflow/intervention}}
\]

Keep physical mass, accounting price, and consequence assumptions separate.

## Required economic inputs

- production-rate trace;
- current and alternative route;
- prime and downgrade/netback values;
- reblend/rework/disposal consequences where applicable;
- sample cost and effective result latency;
- actual decision time;
- final lab/disposition truth;
- false-prime consequence policy;
- model deployment/maintenance costs for scale-up.

## Economic evidence levels

- **Competition POC:** per-episode simulated value only.
- **HMEL offline study:** measured historical replay value with bootstrap confidence intervals.
- **Shadow mode:** prospective recommendation ledger versus actual decisions.
- **Pilot:** realized audited value after approved decisions.

## Treatment of the deck numbers

The deck’s ₹158 Cr can be reproduced arithmetically only if 65,500 t/year and an approximately ₹20/kg downgrade spread plus other losses are accepted. That makes it a scenario, not proof. Likewise, ₹60–102 Cr/year and 45–90 t per transition are not retained as product promises.

Scale-up must use:

\[
V_{\text{annual scenario}}=
N_{\text{eligible transitions}}
\times \operatorname{median}(V_{\text{validated episode}})
\times A_{\text{availability}}
\times A_{\text{adoption}}
\]

with low/base/high inputs, uncertainty intervals, and no double counting. Report both gross opportunity and net value after recurring cost.

L. DECISION ENGINE
Implement a pure, deterministic policy engine.

Expected loss:
EL(a|x) =
  P(material_bad|x) * C_false_prime(a)
  + P(material_good|x) * C_false_hold(a)
  + C_sample(a)
  + C_workflow(a)

Hard gates precede minimization:
- valid transition/spec;
- timestamp alignment;
- critical sensor health;
- no hard abnormality;
- OOD support;
- calibrated interval available;
- material window available;
- dwell rule;
- required approval.

PRIME-RELEASE CANDIDATE:
- never “prime certified”;
- interval/spec policy passes for configured dwell;
- predicted bad probability below risk threshold;
- health/OOD pass;
- material window and route known;
- no blocking abnormality;
- requires human/QC approval.

HOLD:
- evidence insufficient for candidacy but the system remains applicable;
- current route is maintained.

SAMPLE NOW:
- calculate VOI =
  current minimum expected loss
  - expected post-sample minimum loss
  - sample cost;
- recommend only if positive beyond threshold and result can change the current transition decision.

ABSTAIN:
- any hard applicability/health gate fails;
- fallback is explicit SOP/current routing.

Return reason codes that are testable and human-readable.

M. ECONOMIC ENGINE
